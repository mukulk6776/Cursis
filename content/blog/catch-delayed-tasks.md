## What makes a task delayed before its due date arrives?

A deliverable becomes late days before the calendar date passes. In small 5-to-10-person teams, delayed tasks almost always share three early warning signs: no assigned owner, an unresolved review dependency, or zero status movement for multiple workdays.

Waiting for weekly standups hides those signals until recovery costs overtime. Catching slippage early requires scanning blockers daily without forcing teammates into repetitive status check-ins.

## The Ordis Delay Triage framework

When reviewing active sprint work—either manually in Cursis or by asking **Ordis AI Copilot** on Pro and Business plans—check every active task against three criteria:

### 1. Missing owner or ambiguous next step

Every task in **To Do** or **In Progress** needs one accountable person. If an item is unassigned or stuck at a vague milestone like "working on it," identify the concrete next action—for example, "Confirm staging API schema with Mukul before 4 PM."

### 2. Hidden approval and dependency blocks

Many "late" engineering or design tasks are actually blocked waiting on a decision from someone else. Separating execution delay from review delay prevents blame and directs attention straight to the reviewer holding the lock.

### 3. Teammate capacity overload

A task stays untouched when its owner already carries more committed hours than an 8-hour workday allows. Checking available capacity across the roster lets leads spot who has headroom to absorb testing, documentation, or QA before Friday.

## Three interventions that actually recover a sprint

Once a bottleneck surfaces, choose one explicit intervention rather than leaving the due date unchanged:

- **Reduce scope for the first milestone:** Ship core functionality now and split secondary polish into a separate follow-up task.
- **Unblock the approval gate:** Ping the named reviewer directly with a single review link inside the task thread.
- **Reassign to a teammate with free hours:** Check live **Team Pulse** capacity, ask first, and hand off clean context so the new owner does not start from scratch.
